# Smart Cities, Cooler Futures

## Week 1 Check-in

### A Data-Driven Framework for Urban Cooling Prioritisation

---

## 1. Project Idea

Urban heat is becoming an important challenge for European cities.

However, cities differ in their level of heat exposure, population density, urban form and access to cooling infrastructure such as green space and street trees.

The project explores how different urban characteristics can be combined to better understand urban heat vulnerability and support cooling decisions.

---

## 2. Initial Business Question

> **Where should cities act first to reduce urban heat vulnerability, and what type of cooling intervention should they prioritise?**

The initial idea is to compare different European cities and investigate why their cooling needs may differ.

---

## 3. Cities Selected

The project focuses on 10 European cities:

- Amsterdam
- Athens
- Barcelona
- Berlin
- Budapest
- Lisbon
- Madrid
- Milan
- Prague
- Vienna

The cities were selected to provide a varied European sample with different climatic, environmental and urban characteristics.

---

## 4. Initial Analytical Dimensions

The first project framework considers four main dimensions:

| Dimension | Initial Indicator |
|---|---|
| **Heat** | Urban Heat Island intensity |
| **Exposure** | Population density |
| **Urban Form** | Building density and building height |
| **Cooling Capacity** | Green space and street trees |

The main idea is that urban cooling needs cannot be understood from temperature alone.

A city with high heat may face different challenges depending on how many people are exposed, how densely the city is built and how much cooling infrastructure is available.

---

## 5. Data Sources

Several spatial and environmental datasets were identified for the project:

- Copernicus Urban Atlas 2021
- Urban Atlas Street Tree Layer 2021
- JRC / Eurostat population data
- Urban heat / UHI data
- GHS-OBAT building data
- Copernicus impervious-surface data
- Satellite-derived Land Surface Temperature data

These sources provide information about land use, vegetation, population, buildings and urban heat.

---

## 6. Initial Data Preparation

The first stage focuses on collecting, exploring and preparing the different datasets.

Main preparation tasks include:

- Downloading and organising the source data
- Selecting the 10 study areas
- Checking coordinate reference systems
- Preparing city boundaries
- Exploring land-use categories
- Extracting green-space information
- Preparing street-tree data
- Processing population data
- Preparing building information
- Exploring available urban heat data
- Checking how the different spatial datasets can be combined

Because the datasets come from different sources and spatial formats, data preparation and integration are an important part of the project.

---

## 7. Initial Analysis Plan

The first analytical plan is to create comparable indicators for the 10 cities.

The planned workflow is:

**Data Collection → Data Cleaning → Spatial Processing → Indicator Calculation → City Comparison → Cooling Priorities**

The initial indicators will be used to explore differences between cities and investigate which factors may contribute to urban heat vulnerability.
