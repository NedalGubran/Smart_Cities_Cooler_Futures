# Smart Cities, Cooler Futures

## Week 2 Check-in

### A Data-Driven Framework for Urban Cooling Prioritisation

---

## 1. The Urban Heat Challenge

Urban heat does not affect all cities in the same way.

A city may experience high temperatures, but cooling priority also depends on population exposure, urban form and the availability of green infrastructure.

The project therefore asks:

> **Where should cities act first to reduce urban heat vulnerability, and what type of cooling intervention should they prioritise?**

The analysis compares **10 European cities**:

Amsterdam · Athens · Barcelona · Berlin · Budapest · Lisbon · Madrid · Milan · Prague · Vienna

---

## 2. What Did We Analyse?

To compare the cities, six indicators were analysed across four main dimensions:

| Dimension | Indicator |
|---|---|
| **Heat** | UHI Intensity |
| **Exposure** | Population Density |
| **Urban Form** | Building Density + Building Height |
| **Cooling Capacity** | Green Space + Street Trees |

These dimensions represent different aspects of urban heat vulnerability.

> **Urban cooling priority is not determined by temperature alone. It depends on the combination of Heat + Exposure + Urban Form + Cooling Capacity.**

---

## 3. Analytical Approach

### Step 1: Measure Urban Heat Vulnerability

The six indicators were prepared and analysed for all 10 cities.

**Heat + Exposure + Urban Form + Cooling Capacity**

### Step 2: Normalise the Indicators

Because the indicators use different units, they were normalised to a common **0 to 1 risk scale**.

This made the different indicators comparable.

### Step 3: Calculate the Priority Score

The normalised indicators were combined using equal weights to create an **Urban Cooling Priority Score** for each city.

**6 Indicators → Normalisation → Priority Score → Ranking**

### Step 4: Understand the Priority Drivers

The ranking shows where cooling action may be prioritised, but it does not explain why.

Driver Analysis was therefore used to identify the main factors contributing to each city's priority.

### Step 5: Connect Analysis with Cooling Actions

The analytical results were used to begin developing city-specific cooling recommendations based on the main characteristics of each city.

---

## 4. Priority Score Results

The analysis identified the following highest Priority Scores:

1. **Athens: 0.700**
2. **Milan: 0.689**
3. **Amsterdam: 0.588**

These cities rank highly for different reasons:

- **Athens:** UHI Intensity + Population Density
- **Milan:** Green-Space Deficit + UHI Intensity
- **Amsterdam:** Building Density + Green-Space Deficit

> **Different cities can have high cooling priority for different reasons.**

---

## 5. What Did We Learn?

The first results showed that surface heat alone does not explain urban cooling priority.

Different combinations of heat, population, urban form and cooling capacity can produce similar priority levels.

This means that understanding the drivers behind the Priority Score is important before defining cooling interventions.

---
